Diagnostic Method

  1. Verify the complaint and operating conditions.
  2. Check battery voltage, fuses, grounds, and connectors where relevant.
  3. Scan all available modules and record codes before clearing anything.
  4. Test the circuit or system before replacing parts.
  5. Confirm the repair under the original failure conditions.
Do not replace a component based only on a trouble code. A code identifies a detected condition or circuit, not automatically a failed part.

Important Code Notes

Generic OBD-II definitions are only a starting point. Manufacturer-specific descriptions, enabling conditions, wiring diagrams, pinpoint tests, and service bulletins vary by year, make, model, engine, and module.

Record freeze-frame data, fuel trims, misfire counters, and related codes before clearing codes. A basic generic scanner may not access ABS, SRS, transmission, or body modules.

Custom Wiring Checklist

  • Identify the actual load current and wire gauge.
  • Fuse the circuit close to its power source.
  • Use a relay when the control circuit should not carry the load.
  • Verify ground quality with a voltage-drop test under load.
  • Protect wiring from heat, abrasion, water, and moving parts.
  • Document pinouts, fuse sizes, and wire colors.
